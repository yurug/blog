Hand-drawn diagram in the style of a mathematician's whiteboard sketch:
black ink on white, no colour, no shading, no grey fill, no decoration.
Confident freehand line work, slightly wobbly rectangles, slightly curved
arrows, hand-lettered sans-serif labels. Not a mechanical computer-drawn
flowchart.

LEGIBILITY FIRST. The picture will be shown about 800 pixels wide, so every
word must be comfortable to read at that size: large lettering, few words,
generous white space, no label touching a line.

THE PICTURE OBEYS A STRICT VISUAL GRAMMAR. The words ARTIFACT, WORKER, FLOW
and ACTION below are vocabulary for you, the illustrator, describing how to
draw things. THEY ARE NOT LABELS AND MUST NEVER APPEAR ANYWHERE IN THE
PICTURE. Do not write the word ARTIFACT above a box. Do not write the word
WORKER above a box. The only words that may be drawn are the ones in the
final list at the end of this brief. There are exactly three kinds of
box and exactly two kinds of arrow, and nothing in the picture may depart
from them.

BOXES.
- An ARTIFACT is a box drawn with a DOTTED outline. Its name is written
  INSIDE the box, on the first line, in lowercase italics; what it contains
  is listed under the name, one short item per line, in upright lowercase.
  Every artifact box in the picture is drawn this way, with no exception.
- A WORKER is a box drawn with a NORMAL SOLID outline, containing one name
  in upright capitals and nothing else, except where stated below.
- THE AGENT is the single box drawn with a VERY THICK SOLID outline.

ARROWS.
- A FLOW arrow is THICK and carries NO label, ever. It means an artifact
  moves: into the agent, out of the agent, or into a worker that reads it.
- An ACTION arrow is THIN and ALWAYS carries a lowercase verb as its label.
  It means a worker acts on an artifact. Every thin arrow in the picture has
  a verb; no thick arrow has any label. A thin arrow is a SINGLE STROKE of
  the pen with a small simple arrowhead: never a hollow outlined arrow,
  never a double line.

LAYOUT, left to right, five vertical bands, all boxes in a band sharing the
same width, their centres aligned on a common vertical axis.

Band 1, far left, vertically centred: one WORKER box reading "ENGINEER" in
capitals, and directly under that word, inside the same box, one small
ARTIFACT box whose only content is the italic lowercase word
"understanding".

Band 2: three ARTIFACT boxes stacked vertically, of equal width:
- "context" containing "instructions", "files", "tools";
- "environment" containing "codebase", "knowledge base", "infrastructure";
- "goal" containing "what we asked for".

Three THIN ACTION arrows fan out from the right edge of the ENGINEER box,
one to each of these three boxes, each carrying one lowercase verb, placed
clear of the other arrows: "sets" to context, "maintains" to environment,
"writes" to goal. All three are drawn identically, so that the three
relations plainly look like three of the same thing.

Band 3: THE AGENT box, a rounded rectangle of ordinary proportions, WIDER
THAN IT IS TALL, no taller than the "environment" box beside it, centred
vertically against band 2. It must look like the other boxes in shape and
differ from them ONLY by the thickness of its outline: it is not a cylinder,
not a tube, not a tall column, and its top and bottom edges are straight. It
contains "AGENT" in very large capitals and, beneath it on its own line and
smaller, "Ctx x Env x Goal -> Env'". The three arrows arriving from band 2
converge on its left edge. Three THICK FLOW arrows come into
it, one from each band 2 box, all three drawn identically and none labelled.

Band 4: one ARTIFACT box named "new environment" containing "codebase'",
"knowledge base'", "infrastructure'". One THICK FLOW arrow goes from the
AGENT box into it.

Band 5: two WORKER boxes stacked vertically, "DETERMINISTIC CHECKS" on two
lines and "AUDITORS" with the smaller lowercase line "human or AI" beneath

"pass / fail", with one THICK FLOW arrow from each worker box into it.

A single DASHED rounded boundary, the only dashed line in the whole picture,
